# Getting to know research groups

Lab pages help you see who works on a question and how the work develops over time. Pick a project and move between its paper, code, and author pages instead of memorizing lab names.

## Vision, robotics, and embodied AI

- **[Stanford Vision and Learning Lab, SVL](https://svl.stanford.edu/)**: visual understanding and learning, including BEHAVIOR and ObjectFolder. Start with the robot task, then inspect data, perception, and evaluation.
- **[Berkeley RAIL](https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/)**: learning algorithms, robotics, and vision, focused on acquiring flexible behavior. Choose a project under Publications, then find its implementation under Software.
- **[Tsinghua Embodied Intelligence Lab](https://iiis.tsinghua.edu.cn/kxyj/ktzjs/qhdxjsznsys.htm)**: an official university introduction. Follow it toward teams and projects, alongside the [embodied AI materials](embodied.md).
- **[Shanghai Jiao Tong MVIG](https://www.mvig.org/)**: machine vision and intelligence, with research and application information. Follow embodied projects to individual researchers.
- **[Berkeley AI Research, BAIR](https://bair.berkeley.edu/)**: a broader community. Read researchers' explanations in the [blog archive](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/archive/), then find the relevant group.

## Language models and efficient computation

- **[Stanford NLP](https://nlp.stanford.edu/)**: connect questions through papers, members, and teaching materials.
- **[MIT HAN Lab](https://hanlab.mit.edu/)**: efficient AI, compression, deployment, and the interaction of algorithms and systems. Follow work such as AWQ and SmoothQuant for quantization and inference speed.
- **[THUDM / THUKEG open-source organization](https://github.com/THUDM)**: repositories leading to models, evaluation, and papers.

## Industry: original releases and technical material

[OpenAI News](https://openai.com/news/), [Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research), [Google DeepMind publications](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/), and [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/) let you trace a news item to the original work. For relevant topics, inspect tasks, data, evaluation, and limitations.

## After visiting a lab page

Choose a paper whose question interests you and find earlier or later work. Look at the people behind it: student pages often contain blogs, project records, or code with details the paper leaves out.

Before reaching out, organize questions and attempts using [contact and collaboration](contact.md). Read current recruitment conditions: for example, SVL's application entry specifies Stanford students, and MVIG's undergraduate internship entry includes institutional eligibility conditions.
