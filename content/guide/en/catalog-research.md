## Understanding research: reading, thinking, and the unknown

- **[Why are research skills so seldom taught? — Michael Nielsen](https://michaelnielsen.org/blog/why-are-research-skills-so-seldom-taught-v30/)** — An English essay drawing on physics research to compare research skills with classroom problem-solving and discuss how researchers develop a way of working.

- **[Advice to a Beginning Graduate Student — Manuel Blum](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mblum/research/pdf/grad.html)** — English advice on reading, thinking, research, and writing. Begin with top-down reading: skip ahead around a question, then fill knowledge gaps.

- **[A Survival Guide to a PhD — Andrej Karpathy](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/)** — A 2016 personal blog about research, independence, and sustained effort during a PhD. Start with daily research and ways of working.

- **[The importance of stupidity in scientific research — Martin A. Schwartz](https://web.stanford.edu/~fukamit/schwartz-2008.pdf)** — A one-page English essay from 2008 about moving from questions with known answers into genuine uncertainty. Useful when beginning research.

- **[How Do I Get Started in Research? — Stanford Academic Advising](https://advising.stanford.edu/current-students/advising-student-handbook/start-research)** — Undergraduate advice on starting from interests, faculty conversations, and projects. Institutional programs are intended for Stanford students.

## Growth, university life, and asking for help

- **[Shanghai Jiao Tong University Survival Manual](https://github.com/SurviveSJTU/SurviveSJTUManual)** — A Chinese collective account of learning, university environments, and personal choices, grounded in SJTU students' experiences.

- **[How To Ask Questions The Smart Way, Chinese translation](https://github.com/ryanhanwu/How-To-Ask-Questions-The-Smart-Way)** — Technical communication advice. Consult how to explain goals, attempts, environment, and evidence when writing a help request or GitHub issue.

- **[Principles of Effective Research — Michael Nielsen](https://michaelnielsen.org/blog/principles-of-effective-research-part-i/)** — A long English personal essay about how time, habits, and self-development influence long-term research.

- **[Crash Course Philosophy](https://thecrashcourse.com/topic/philosophy/)** — Short English philosophy videos. Choose topics in knowledge, values, and ethics to explore questions beyond your university courses.

## Finding papers, tracing sources, and saving material

- **[Google Scholar Search Help](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/help.html)** — Official help for searching by author and title, following citations and related papers, and using search syntax.

- **[AMiner platform guide](https://docs.aminer.cn/user-guide/platform-overview/)** — Chinese product documentation for scholarly search, Q&A, and reading. Learn the features and follow citations back to papers.

- **[Zotero: The Basics](https://www.zotero.org/support/quick_start_guide)** — Official introduction to saving, organizing, and citing sources. Start with collections, items, and attachments.

- **[OpenReview documentation](https://docs.openreview.net/)** — Official documentation for paper versions, public reviews, author responses, and related functions.

- **[Hugging Face Daily Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers)** — An entry to daily papers and discussion. Follow interesting work to the original paper, code, and project.

- **[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/)**｜Local Markdown notes and links between papers, questions, and projects.

- **[Zotero Integration](https://github.com/community-archive/obsidian-zotero-integration)**｜Import Zotero citations, notes, and PDF annotations into Obsidian; setup is documented in the repository.

- **[Zotero Connector](https://www.zotero.org/download/connectors)**｜Save bibliographic information and available attachments from your browser.

- **[Scholaread 靠岸学术](https://www.scholaread.cn/help)**｜Paper reading, translation, and notes; irene uses it alongside Obsidian.

- **[切问 / Qiewen](https://qiewenpaper.com/zh/home)**｜Search for papers from a research question, then read and compare related work.

## Reading a first paper and understanding publication

- **[How to Read a Paper — S. Keshav](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~brecht/courses/854-Experimental-Performance-Evaluation-2018/readings/common/how-to-read/keshav-paper-reading.pdf)** — A two-page English guide to three-pass reading: establish what a paper does, then decide how deeply to read. Try it with a paper beside you.

- **[Mu Li: paper-reading index](https://github.com/mli/paper-reading)** — Chinese close-reading videos. Start with how to read a paper, then watch how he examines an abstract, figures, experiments, and structure in a paper you are studying. [Transformer close reading](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1pu411o7BE)

- **[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/)** — A natural-language-processing paper archive. Search by title, author, or venue for papers, versions, and citation information.

- **[Proceedings of Machine Learning Research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/)** — ML conference and workshop proceedings. Find formal papers, supplementary material, and related work from the same event.

## Experiments, tuning, diagnosis, and evaluation

- **[A Recipe for Training Neural Networks — Karpathy](https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/)** — English training experience from 2019. A diagnosis sequence built around inspecting data, starting simply, and checking fit.

- **[Deep Learning Tuning Playbook — Google Research](https://github.com/google-research/tuning_playbook)** — An English tuning handbook. Consult initial setup, experiment organization, and optimization once you are running experiments.

- **[scikit-learn: Common pitfalls and recommended practices](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html)** — Official examples of leakage, inconsistent preprocessing, and randomness, with incorrect and recommended approaches side by side.

- **[Task-Specific LLM Evals that Do & Don't Work — Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/evals/)** — An English practical blog on choosing what and how to evaluate for a specific LLM task. Useful when an application already exists and needs evaluation.

- **[Jupyter](https://jupyter.org/)**｜Keep code, analysis, tables, and figures together in a notebook.

- **[Rainbow CSV](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=mechatroner.rainbow-csv)**｜Distinguish CSV/TSV columns and check column consistency in VS Code.

## Writing, LaTeX, Markdown, and presentation

- **[How to write a great research paper — Simon Peyton Jones](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/write-great-research-paper/)** — English talk and slides on communicating the problem, contribution, and argument. Useful while structuring a paper.

- **[MLNLP Paper-Writing-Tips](https://github.com/MLNLP-World/Paper-Writing-Tips)** — A Chinese writing checklist for checking format, expression, and submission details once you have a draft.

- **[Learn LaTeX in 30 minutes — Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/learn/latex/Learn_LaTeX_in_30_minutes)** — An introductory tutorial covering text, equations, figures, and citations before applying a venue template.

- **[Markdown Guide: Basic Syntax](https://www.markdownguide.org/basic-syntax/)** — A syntax reference for headings, links, lists, images, and code blocks, useful for documents and GitHub READMEs.

- **[How to give a great research talk — Simon Peyton Jones and others](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/give-great-research-talk/)** — Talks and slides, including a Chinese slide entry, on organizing information around the audience and planning a research presentation.

- **[Fundamentals of Data Visualization — Claus O. Wilke](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/)** — An open illustrated book. Consult chart selection, colors, and readability for the figure you are making.

- **[AstraDraw](https://github.com/AstraDraw/astradraw)** — A collaborative whiteboard and presentation project marked Alpha in its repository. Try it for organizing diagrams and presentations.

- **[LaTeX Workshop](https://github.com/James-Yu/LaTeX-Workshop)**｜Build and preview LaTeX and complete citations in VS Code with a local TeX installation.

- **[Code Spell Checker](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=streetsidesoftware.code-spell-checker)**｜Check English spelling in notes, READMEs, and code comments.

## AI in research: tasks, checks, and reuse

- **[Building effective agents — Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)** — An English engineering article from 2024 comparing fixed workflows and autonomous agents, and extending simple structures gradually.

- **[How I use LLMs — Karpathy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWvNQjAaOHw)** — An English video demonstrating LLM use in everyday tasks; a concrete collection of tool-use examples.

- **[Building A Generative AI Platform — Chip Huyen](https://huyenchip.com/2024/07/25/genai-platform.html)** — An English engineering blog connecting data, models, retrieval, and evaluation from a systems perspective. Read after building a simple generative-AI application.

- **[Full Stack Deep Learning](https://fullstackdeeplearning.com/)** — Courses, talks, and engineering resources covering data, development, and deployment beyond the model. Choose by your current project stage.
